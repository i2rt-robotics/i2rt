# 7-DOF Arm (left) Physical Properties

This document records the physical and kinematic properties of the **left** seven-DOF arm from [`yam_7dof_left.urdf`](yam_7dof_left.urdf). The corresponding MuJoCo model is [`yam_7dof_left.xml`](yam_7dof_left.xml).

The left arm is not a separate design. It is [`../../yam_7dof_right/v1/`](../../yam_7dof_right/v1/) **reflected about the world x-z plane** (`y → -y`), generated by `.claude/skills/align-urdf-mjcf/scripts/mirror_arm_model.py --name yam_7dof_left`, which re-runs whenever the right model changes. The `--name` is what gives the mirrored files their own family name, which the path resolver and `sync_gripper_mounts.py` both key off. The two sides are the same hardware; the reflection describes the same arm mounted on the opposite face of the mounting plane.

Everything in [`../../yam_7dof_right/v1/README.md`](../../yam_7dof_right/v1/README.md) about scope, units, conventions and provenance applies here unchanged — including the gripper-mount spigot, which the mirror carries across with the rest of `wrist.stl`, and its gravity comp and motor torque registers (`KT_Value`/`GREF`, checked at startup), which [`config/yam_7dof_left_v1.yml`](../../../../robots/config/yam_7dof_left_v1.yml) shares with the right arm's. This document records only what the reflection changes.

## The reflection

With `M = diag(1, -1, 1)`, every quantity maps by a sign rule. Each rule is a sign flip or a component swap, so the mirror is **exact**: no number is re-rounded, and the mirrored STL coordinates differ from the source only in their float32 sign bit.

| Quantity | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Any position — link frames, COM, joint origins, mesh placements | negate `y` |
| Any frame orientation — URDF `rpy`, MJCF `quat` | negate roll and yaw, keep pitch (`R → M R M`) |
| MJCF principal-axis `<inertial quat>` | `(w,x,y,z) → (-y,z,w,-x)`, i.e. left-multiplied by `Ry(π)` |
| Inertia products `Ixy`, `Iyz` | negate |
| Hinge axis — a pseudovector | negate `x` and `z` (`a → -M·a`) |
| Slide axis — an ordinary vector | negate `y` (`a → M·a`) |
| Mass, principal moments, `Ixx`/`Iyy`/`Izz`/`Ixz`, link lengths, **joint ranges** | unchanged |
| STL triangle | negate `y` of the normal and all three vertices, then swap two vertices to restore CCW winding |

Frame orientations and principal-axis triads take *different* quaternion rules because a frame is conjugated by `M` while a principal triad is only left-multiplied by it. `M·R_p` has determinant −1 and is therefore not a rotation; `-M = Ry(π)` is the left-multiplication that re-rights it while leaving `diaginertia` untouched, which is why the principal moments below are bit-identical to the right arm's.

## Joint sign convention

**An identical joint vector puts the two arms in mirror-symmetric poses:** `q_left = q_right`, joint for joint. That falls out of the axis rules above rather than being imposed on top of them, and it is why the joint ranges come through the reflection untouched.

A rotation axis is a pseudovector, transforming as `a → det(M)·M·a = -M·a`; a translation axis is an ordinary vector, `a → M·a`. Substituting either into the mirrored frame `R' = M·R·M` reproduces the reflected motion at the *same* joint value — so nothing about the joint coordinate, its limits, or `directions` needs re-signing.

In world terms the pseudovector rule is what makes the two sides' specified home directions differ the way they do: an axis along the mirror **normal** keeps its world direction, one lying **in** the mirror plane reverses.

| Joint | Right arm | Left arm | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `joint1`, `joint4`, `joint7` | `-y` | `-y` | axis along the mirror normal — unchanged |
| `joint2`, `joint6` | `-x` | `+x` | axis in the mirror plane — reversed |
| `joint3`, `joint5` | `-z` | `+z` | axis in the mirror plane — reversed |

`test_left_is_the_mirror_of_right_when_posed` in [`test_arm_7dof.py`](../../../../robots/tests/test_arm_7dof.py) pins the relation by feeding both models the same `q` — at posed configurations, because at the home pose the two arms are reflections of each other whatever their axes say, and every sign error hides there.

`directions` in [`config/yam_7dof_left_v1.yml`](../../../../robots/config/yam_7dof_left_v1.yml) is `[-1, +1, -1, +1, -1, -1, +1]`: the right arm's with `joint3`, `joint5` and `joint6` flipped (2026-10-12). Those three motors do not reverse their positive rotation across the reflection the way their axes do, so the model-to-motor relation differs from the right arm's there, while the model itself stays a true mirror image. See the right README's "Joint sign convention" for why the mixed sign belongs in `directions` rather than in the model.

It is nonetheless **not yet bench-verified** — nothing here has been checked against a physical left arm. Jog each joint ±0.1 rad against `--sim` before trusting it; if one runs backwards, flipping its entry there is the correct fix, unlike a model/hardware sign mismatch, which has to be fixed in the model since a `directions` flip reaches neither the model's joint limits nor `SimRobot`.

## Summary

Mass properties are reflection invariant, so the totals are the right arm's exactly.

| Property | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Arm DOF | 7 |
| Gripper DOF | 1 (two coupled fingers) |
| Arm bodies, `base` through `gripper` | 8 (the last is the mount frame) |
| Arm mass, `base` through `link6` | 5.843760 kg |
| Total mass, composed with `linear_4310` | 6.538987 kg |
| Sum of parent-to-child translation norms | 0.8217221650 m |

Joint travel is shared too: the two sides' `range` values are identical, because mirror-symmetric axes leave the joint coordinate alone.

The mounting face still lies in the world x-z plane and the arm still hangs along `-Z` at home, but the face's outward normal is now `-Y` and the body sits on the `+Y` side.

## Product-of-exponentials screw axes

Space-frame (base-frame) screw axes for the Modern Robotics PoE formulation

```text
T(θ) = e^([S₁]θ₁) · e^([S₂]θ₂) ··· e^([S₇]θ₇) · M
```

where each `Sᵢ = (ωᵢ, vᵢ)` with `ωᵢ` the unit joint axis and `vᵢ = -ωᵢ × qᵢ` (`qᵢ` a point on the axis), all at the home configuration. The end-effector frame is the `gripper` frame; the space frame is the `base` frame.

Every `ωᵢ` matches the right arm's, by construction. The moment terms `vᵢ` are what the reflection moves.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `joint1` | `0 -1 0` | `0 0 -0.000115936` |
| `joint2` | `1 0 0` | `0 0 -0.1565` |
| `joint3` | `0 0 1` | `0.156501 -0.000117156 0` |

```text
[ +0.000001  +1.000000  -0.000004  +0.000209 ]
[ -0.000000  +0.000004  +1.000000  +0.130849 ]
[ +1.000000  -0.000001  +0.000000  -0.579404 ]
[ +0.000000  +0.000000  +0.000000  +1.000000 ]
```

```text
Bᵢ   = [Ad_{M⁻¹}] Sᵢ
```
